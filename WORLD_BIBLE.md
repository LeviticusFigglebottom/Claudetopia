## 1. Cosmology: the Dwindling, four ways

Nobody in Wickmere agrees on what the world is. Everyone agrees on what it is
doing: **dwindling**. Colour thins at the edges, sound dies sooner in the deep
places, the dead sometimes fail to leave, and villages that stop being talked
about stop being found. Four accounts survive. Each predicts some things and
fails at others. Books in the game quote all four and contradict each other.
The truth is never stated outright; the final quest lets the player *act* on one.

### 1.1 The Bell (the Old Builders' account, in Oroth)
The world is the ringing of a bell struck once, before time. Sound is being.
Where the ringing is loud there is warmth and colour; where it fades, things go
still and "unsaid". The Builders (the Oroth) tried to make the ringing eternal by
raising **Harmonies**: cathedral-machines tuned to sustain the note. They failed.
Their greatest work, the **Cantor's Seat**, still holds a note, but the wrong one:
a note that keeps its holder from ending. The dead in the deep places are held in
that note. *Predicts*: deep places are quiet and cold; the Toll hums when the note
shifts; bells matter. *Fails*: cannot explain why gossip warms a village.

### 1.2 The Candle (the Vale account, in Valish)
The world is a candle, the sun its flame, and every hearth a small copy of it.
A flame goes out when nobody tends it. "Tending" is everything done with care and
in company: cooking, mending, naming children, telling stories about your
neighbours. Gossip is a kind of prayer. A person nobody speaks of goes quiet and
one day is not there. *Predicts*: renown anchors; villages that talk stay warm.
*Fails*: cannot explain the Builders' works, which are cold and enormous and
still stand.

### 1.3 The Breath (the Skerrow clans' account, in Skerrish)
Something vast breathed the world out, and is now breathing in. What lies nearest
the mouth goes first: the very old, the very deep, the forgotten. The giants who
raised the mountains were breathed in long ago; their bones are what is left when
a thing is taken back but its weight is not. Clans keep long memories because
memory is a hand on the rope. *Predicts*: giant bones, why the oldest places fail
first. *Fails*: cannot explain why the Cantor's Seat has *not* gone.

### 1.4 The Tide (the Sedgemire account, in Sedgish)
The world is a shore and the Dwindling is the tide going out. What is left on the
sand is strange and beautiful and does not belong to the air. Things return with
the flood. The marsh folk do not fear the going-out: they build stilts, they wait,
they fish what is stranded. Some among them believe the flood is coming back and
that the drowned will walk. *Predicts*: cyclical hope; the strangeness of the deep
places. *Fails*: there has never been a flood in any record.

### 1.5 What is consistent across all four (the design truth)
* **Being known holds.** Renown, names, songs, records and witnessed deeds
  literally keep people and places from fading. This is why Hearthstones keep
  the player, why the player's Echo waits where they were last known, and why
  gossip spreads reputation as a mechanic.
* **The deep places failed first.** They are where the Builders' works held on a
  wrong note, or where nobody went for too long.
* **The Hushline is where it has finished.** South of Cinderlea colour and sound
  drain away. The player came out of it, which should be impossible.

Nobody worships a god by name. There are **the Named**: figures remembered so
long that they became more than people. Hearth-Mother Ansel, who first told a
story about a neighbour; Old Kharrow, who carried a mountain's name up a cliff;
Isse of the Reeds, who taught the stilts; the Cantor, whose name is not said.

## 2. History (as far as anyone agrees)

Dates are given in **Y.T.** (*Years of the Toll*), counted from the day the
Cracked Toll fell silent. Now is **Y.T. 1043**.

| Age | Span | What happened (contested details in *italics*) |
|---|---|---|
| **The Loud Age** | before −2000 | The Oroth (the Old Builders) raise Harmonies across the whole basin. Tollmere's island is their foundation stone. Giants walk Skerrow. *The Oroth may have been human; the clans say they were not.* |
| **The Raising of the Briar** | c. −900 | The Oroth grow the Thornmarch along the eastern rim. *To keep something out (Oroth books) or to keep the Dwindling in (Sedgish songs).* |
| **The Wrong Note** | c. −300 | The Cantor's Seat is completed. The Choir sings. Something goes wrong; the Sunken Choir statues lose their heads in one night. The Oroth begin to vanish from the record. |
| **The Fall of the Toll** | 0 | The great bell above what is now Merrowby cracks and falls from its tower, which no longer exists. First recorded human settlements in Hearthvale in the same decade. *Coincidence, or the bell's fall made the valley habitable.* |
| **The Tending** | 1–400 | Vale folk learn that talked-of villages last. Festival calendar established. Merrowby founded around the bell. Isseva raised on stilts. |
| **The Chain Years** | 400–700 | Skerrow clans forge the chain bridges and open Windgate Pass to trade. The Fallen Hand is found when the Oskeld glacier retreats. |
| **The Guild Charter** | 712 | Tollmere's guilds (Tallymen, Sayers, later the Quiet Hands in secret) write the Charter. Law becomes a paid service. |
| **The Ash Winter** | 866 | Cinderlea burns for a year. Greyfold's people walk south into the Hush singing. The Tolling Order is founded by the ones who turned back. |
| **The Quiet Villages** | 990–1040 | Three hamlets on the fringes "go quiet": found empty, then not found at all. Only the Wardens' rolls remember their names. |
| **Now** | 1043 | The Cracked Toll hums for the first time in living memory. A Foundling walks out of the Hushline. |

## 3. Peoples and cultures

There is one human people in Wickmere with five cultures. No fantasy races: the
difference between a Vale baker and a Skerrow cliff-warden is what they remember,
what they fear, and how they cook.

### 3.1 The Vale folk (Hearthvale; Valish)
Farmers, millers, brewers, gossips. Warm, nosy, quick to judge and quick to
forgive if you bring a pie. Houses of chalk-cob and thatch with painted doors
(door colour = family trade: blue miller, red smith, green grower, yellow
brewer). Festivals: **Naming Day** (spring), **Long Table** (harvest), **Candle
Night** (midwinter, every window lit). Virtue: tending. Vice: talk that curdles
into cruelty. Attitude to the player: measured entirely by renown and Hearth.

Merrowby keeps **seventeen family bells**, one per household of the founding rolls, hung
in trees and over doors. At the Long Table a family whose line has ended has its bell
buried under the table's foot rather than rehung, so the village can say it is still
there. The baker sets the first loaf of the day on the Toll's rim-stone and leaves it
until noon; nobody will say why, and everybody does it.

### 3.2 The Lakefolk of Tollmere (Brightwater; Valish with Oroth loanwords)
Guildsmen, clerks, fishers, watchmen, thieves. Believe in charters and ledgers.
White-lime walls, slate roofs, brass lamps, canals. Tollmere sits on an Oroth
foundation nobody can dig through. Virtue: fairness by the book. Vice: everything
has a price, including law. Attitude: measured by faction rank and bounty.

### 3.3 The Reedfolk (Sedgemire; Sedgish)
Eel-fishers, boatwrights, lantern-makers, patient. Live on stilts and boardwalks,
sing rounds while working. Dye everything with marsh indigo. Bury their dead in
the water with a lantern. Virtue: patience. Vice: fatalism. Attitude: wary of
loud strangers, warm to anyone who waits.

### 3.4 The Clans of Skerrow (Skerrow Heights; Skerrish)
Herders, miners, chain-smiths, memory-keepers. Seven clans, one Moot. Names are
inherited with duties. Blood-price instead of jail. Carve holds into cliff faces,
hang bridges on chains, wear giant-bone tokens. Virtue: memory. Vice: grudges as
long as memory. Attitude: measured by deeds they have *heard* about (renown) and
by honour (no theft from a host).

### 3.5 The Woodfolk of the Briarwold (Briarwold; Valish dialect with old roots)
Foresters, hunters, charcoal-burners, hermits. Live inside the Grandfather and in
lodges. Keep the Standing Moot's stones oiled. Do not have law; have custom, and
bows. Virtue: restraint. Vice: silence that lets wrongs stand. Attitude: distrust
anyone who takes more than they need.

### 3.6 The Ash-Pilgrims (Cinderlea; mixed)
Not a culture but a remnant: pilgrims, the Tolling Order, hermits, the mad.
Wear grey. Speak softly. Count bells.

### 3.6a Two names that matter more than their bearers

* **Tessane** (Sedgish-born, Y.T. 902–941?), a Sayer who taught the Tide account in
  Tollmere and was **unsaid** by the Circle: her name struck from the rolls, her lectures
  burned, her students reassigned. She appears in four surviving books, three of which do
  not name her. Whether being unsaid killed her, or she simply left for Isseva, is the
  question the Sayers' line turns on.
* **Ghorr**, the Bone Clan. Skerrish *gh-* + *orr* (bone). The eighth clan, whose verse is
  missing from the Rope-Song the memory-keepers sing: seven clans, eight verses' worth of
  metre. The Stone-Thrall King has been holding the missing verse, which is to say the
  clan's name, which is to say the clan.

### 3.7 The Oroth (gone)
The Old Builders. Left Harmonies, statues, roads of fused stone, doors with no
hinges, and a language of liturgy. Whether they were people is contested.

### 4.1 Questline shapes (pass one)
* **Wardens**: *The Roll of Names* — recover the roll of a quiet village; find
  who erased a name; choose to restore a bandit's name (redemption) or strike it
  (safety). Ends with the player as **Roll-Warden**, able to name a Hearthstone.
* **Sayers**: *Louder* — three lectures that contradict; retrieve an Oroth bell
  from the Drowned Nave; discover the Circle has been *quieting* a rival's name
  for a century. Ends with the player choosing which account the Circle teaches.

  Tallymen's ledger that prices the law; decide whether to publish it.
* **Tolling Order**: *Vigil* — stand a night watch at the Choir; escort a
  pilgrim into the Hushline and bring back what she leaves; learn what the Cantor
  is. Ends at the gate of the Cantor's Seat.

## 5. Naming languages

Four languages give the map its texture. Generators for each live in
`tools/namegen.py` and follow these rules.

### 5.1 Valish (Common)
Storybook English-adjacent. Places are compounds of a root and a feature suffix.
* **Roots** (meaning): *merrow* (calm water), *hollin* (holly), *tam* (oak),
  *wyn* (meadow), *cad* (hill), *brae* (slope), *thorn*, *ash*, *penny* (small),
  *gos* (goose), *lark*, *fenn*, *hare*, *mull* (soft soil), *cress*, *bram*.
* **Suffixes**: *-by* (village), *-wick* (dwelling), *-combe* (valley),
  *-mere* (lake), *-stead*, *-ford*, *-well*, *-bourne* (stream), *-down* (chalk
  hill), *-hithe* (landing), *-fold* (pen).
* **People**: two syllables; softened endings. Women often *-a, -ie, -el, -ow*;
  men often *-am, -ick, -ard, -en*; many names are used for anyone. Surnames from
  trade or place: Miller, Ashdown, Pennywort, Cresswell, Tallow, Brambling.
* Examples: Merrowby, Tamwick, Ashcombe, Gullhithe, Hollin Barrow, Larkbourne.
  People: Wren Tallow, Osric Pennywort, Maud Brambling, Tobin Ashdown.

### 5.2 Oroth (the Builders' liturgy)
Open syllables (CV, CVC), liquids and nasals, long vowels; endings *-eth, -ar,
-ion, -ael, -ost*. Words: *oroth* (the note / a held sound), *vael* (kept,
held), *hesk* (silence), *isse* (water, sunken), *cantor* (holder; the one who
keeps the note; the Builders' title, not ours), *thael* (door), *morn* (stone),
*anthe* (choir, many voices), *sul* (light), *ondr* (deep).
* Compounds place the modifier second: *Vael-Oroth* "the Kept Note"; *Isse-Anthe*
  "the Drowned Choir" (the Nave); *Morn-Thael* "the Stone Door".
* Examples: Ilmareth, Vaelost, Sulion, Ondrael, Hesk-Morn.

### 5.3 Skerrish (the clans)
Clusters *kh, rr, dd, gh, sk, br*; vowels *a, o, u*; short words, stress on
the first syllable. Places end in *-ow* (hold), *-crag*, *-eld* (mine), *-gate*
(pass), *-fell*. Personal names carry the clan name after **ko-** ("child of"):
*Brann ko-Dreugh*. Clan names: Dreugh, Kharrow, Oskel, Brindle, Ghast, Rudd,
Skarl.
* Words: *skerr* (broken stone), *ghal* (chain), *dun* (remembered), *brakh*
  (giant), *orr* (bone), *fell* (high moor).
* Examples: Kharrow Hold, Brindlecrag, Oskeld, Windgate, Ghastfell, Ruddow.
  People: Brann ko-Dreugh, Ushka ko-Kharrow, Tam ko-Rudd (a Vale name adopted).

### 5.4 Sedgish (the reedfolk)
Soft, sibilant, vowel-final; doubled consonants for length; the glottal
apostrophe appears only in names of the drowned. Places end in *-eva* (landing),
*-oul* (pool), *-issa* (reed-bed), *-nauve* (channel).
* Words: *isse* (water; shared with Oroth, a clue), *sa* (lantern), *lissa*
  (reed), *nauve* (channel), *mor* (eel), *tal* (patience), *oul* (deep pool).
* Examples: Isseva, Eelfathom (Valish translation of *Mor-Oul*), Lissane,
  Saoul, Nauve's Landing. People: Issa Tal, Lissane, Nauve Mor, Sa'oul (drowned).

### 5.5 Naming rules for content authors
* A place name must be pronounceable by the culture that named it. Oroth ruins
  in Sedgemire have both an Oroth and a Sedgish name; the map shows the local one.
* No real-world place or deity names. `tools/namegen.py --check` flags collisions
  with a banned list.

## 6. Region identity sheets

Each sheet is also data in `content/packs/core/regions/<id>.json`. The
**drop test**: with the HUD off, a player should know the region within seconds.
Each region's "Palette & light" line below is carried out as a named light
(Harvest Gold, Lake Glass, Drowned Lantern, Green Cathedral, Bone and Slate,
Ember Ash), written up with what each is for in `game/systems/atmosphere/README.md`
("Six lights"), and its values are the region's `identity.light`.

### 6.1 Hearthvale (`core:region/hearthvale`) — the start
* **Geology & landforms**: chalk downland; long whale-backed hills, dry valleys,
  a clear chalk stream (the Larkbourne) with watercress; a white scar where the
  hill was cut by the falling Toll.
* **Palette & light**: gold, clover green, chalk white, hedge dark-green, apple
  red, thatch ochre. Warm sun 40° with long afternoon light; soft bloom; fog
  pale gold in the mornings.
* **Weather**: clear 45%, overcast 25%, soft rain 20%, morning mist 10%.
* **Flora**: oaks in hedgerows, apple orchards in rows, hawthorn, cow parsley,
  poppies in barley, single yews in barrows.
* **Architecture**: chalk-cob walls, thatch, painted doors by trade, wattle
  fences, a stone mill, dovecotes.
* **Culture**: Vale folk (§3.1). Wardens keep the rolls.
* **Enemy ecology**: bandits at the road margins (skirmisher/pack), wolves in
  the dry valleys (pack), bristleback boars (charger), hedge-wights in barrows
  (brute), crows as witnesses.
* **Soundscape**: skylarks, bees, a mill wheel, church-less bells (the Toll's
  small cousins hung in trees), fiddles at the inn.
* **Landmark**: **the Cracked Toll**, a bronze bell 40 m tall half-buried in the
  hill above Merrowby, its lip a cave, its crown a lookout. Visible from every
  part of the region and from the Mere.
* **Unique things**: the only apple trees in Wickmere; the Chalk Hound cut into
  the down; the Wardens' Roll (a book that lists names that no longer exist).

### 6.2 Brightwater & the Mere (`core:region/brightwater`)
* **Geology**: a wide lake in an Oroth-shaped basin; shingle shores, reed
  fringes to the west, low cliffs to the north; Tollmere island of fused black
  stone joined by the **Long Stride** causeway.
* **Palette & light**: lake blue, lime white, slate grey, brass, terracotta.
  High clear light, hard shadows at noon, glittering water; blue-grey haze.
* **Weather**: clear 55%, breezy overcast 25%, squalls 15%, fog 5%.
* **Flora**: pollarded willows, limes along the quay, market herbs, waterlilies.
* **Architecture**: lime-washed walls, slate, brass lamps, canals with iron
  bridges, guild halls with charter-boards, the Sayers' Spire (Oroth-built,
  human-topped), the Lamp lighthouse.
* **Culture**: Lakefolk (§3.2).
* **Enemy ecology**: cutpurses (skirmisher), gutter drakes in the Undercroft
  (swarm), smugglers (skirmisher/caster), the lake serpent (boss), hired
  bravos (elite).
* **Soundscape**: gulls, bells on buoys, market cries, water slapping stone,
  ledger-clerks' bells.
* **Landmark**: **the Sayers' Spire** (black Oroth stalk with a brass crown of
  human construction) and **the Lamp**, whose light sweeps the lake at night.
* **Unique things**: the only city; canals; a hinge-less Oroth door in the
  Undercroft that opens for names; the Ledger of Prices.

### 6.3 Sedgemire (`core:region/sedgemire`)
* **Geology**: delta marsh; reed beds, meandering channels, peat islands,
  brackish tide-flats to the west, sunken Oroth masonry everywhere.
* **Palette & light**: teal, reed gold, indigo, bruise purple, lantern amber.
  Low diffuse light, thick low fog, lanterns visible far off; bioluminescent
  wisps at night.
* **Weather**: fog 40%, drizzle 25%, overcast 20%, rare clear 15%.
* **Flora**: reeds, willows, alder carr, marsh marigold, bladderwort, sundew.
* **Architecture**: stilts, boardwalks, rope bridges, indigo-dyed cloth,
  lantern poles, upturned boats as roofs, water-burial jetties.
* **Culture**: Reedfolk (§3.3).
* **Enemy ecology**: the bog-drowned (brute/swarm, slow, grabbing), sallowjaws
  (ambusher from water), wisps (caster/lure), leech-hounds (pack), the marsh-hag
  (elite caster), She Who Waits in the Nave (boss).
* **Soundscape**: frogs, bitterns, dripping, rope creak, rounds sung across water,
  a submerged bell now and then.
* **Landmark**: **the Drowned Nave** (*Isse-Anthe*), an Oroth spire leaning 15°
  out of the marsh, 120 m tall, its lower half flooded.
* **Unique things**: stilt-town; lantern water-burials; the only indigo; the
  Eelfathom Pool that shows a reflection a few seconds behind.

### 6.4 The Briarwold (`core:region/briarwold`)
* **Geology**: old forest on rising ground over granite; ravines with
  waterfalls, mossy boulders, root-caves, the Briar wall on the eastern rim.
* **Palette & light**: deep green, black-ash bark, amber light shafts, moss
  lime, bracken rust, fungus violet. Dim under canopy with volumetric shafts;
  clearings blaze.
* **Weather**: still 40%, rain 30% (heard more than seen), mist 20%, storm 10%.
* **Flora**: colossal trunks (our own generated giants), ferns, bracken,
  foxglove, bracket fungus, hanging moss, the Briar (thorn vines thick as
  arms).
* **Architecture**: wood-carved lodges, rope walks in the Grandfather, bark
  shingles, antler hooks, stone circles kept oiled.
* **Culture**: Woodfolk (§3.5).
* **Enemy ecology**: thornhounds (pack), weaver spiders (ambusher from above),
  wardens (sentinel treants, slow, huge poise), poachers (skirmisher), Hart-
  Knights (elite, antlered armour), the Hart of Thorns (boss).
* **Soundscape**: wind in high canopy, woodpeckers, distant falls, creaks, an
  owl at noon (wrong, and meant to be).
* **Landmark**: **the Grandfather**, a dead tree 90 m tall and 30 m across,
  hollow, with a town inside and lights in its knots.
* **Unique things**: the tree-town; the Briar; a stone circle that hums when you
  stand in the middle (the Standing Moot); Weaverdeep's silk bridges.

### 6.5 Skerrow Heights (`core:region/skerrow`)
* **Geology**: karst limestone: pavements, sinkholes, gorges, terraced
  waterfalls, high moor with snow on the tops; mines in the veins; giant bones
  in the scree.
* **Palette & light**: slate blue, bone white, heather purple, rust, snow.
  Cold clear light, long shadows, wind-blown cloud shadows racing across moor.
* **Weather**: wind 35%, clear-cold 30%, snow 20% (upper), rain 15%.
* **Flora**: heather, hardy pines, juniper, lichen, cottongrass, no trees above
  the line.
* **Architecture**: cliff-carved holds, chain bridges, drystone, turf roofs,
  bone lintels, mine headframes.
* **Culture**: Clans (§3.4).
* **Enemy ecology**: crag-wolves (pack), scree-hags (ambusher/caster, cliff
  dwellers), stone-thralls (brute, animated bone-and-rock), clanless raiders
  (elite), the Stone-Thrall King (boss), Frostmother (boss, ice cave).
* **Soundscape**: wind, chains, waterfalls, distant hammering, pipes at night.
* **Landmark**: **the Fallen Hand**, a giant's stone hand 60 m long, fingers
  tall as towers, palm up, a clan shrine in its cup.
* **Unique things**: chain bridges; giant bones you can walk inside; the only
  snow; blood-price law; the Oskeld mine's singing shaft.

### 6.6 Cinderlea (`core:region/cinderlea`)
* **Geology**: ash heath over the Builders' city; black soil, grey grass, fused
  stone roads, sunken plazas, the Choir ring, cliffs down to the Hushline.
* **Palette & light**: ash grey, char black, bone, faded gold (old gilding),
  a single cold blue (the Seat's note-light). Low sun always near the horizon,
  desaturated, long fog, drifting ash motes, film grain.
* **Weather**: ashfall 35%, still grey 35%, dry wind 20%, rare thin sun 10%.
* **Flora**: literal ash trees, dead and white; grey grass; one red poppy per
  square kilometre (placed by hand; players notice).
* **Architecture**: Oroth monumental (headless colossi, hinge-less doors, bells
  the size of houses), pilgrim tents, the Order's chapter-house in a bell.
* **Culture**: Ash-Pilgrims, the Tolling Order (§3.6).
* **Enemy ecology**: ash-wights (swarm, easily staggered, many), Tolling
  knights gone wrong (elite duelists), bell-bearers (mini-boss, AoE stun),
  choristers (caster), the Last Cantor (final boss).
* **Soundscape**: near silence, ash hiss, one bell every few minutes, your own
  footsteps too loud, a sustained note underneath everything near the Seat.
* **Landmark**: **the Sunken Choir**, twelve headless colossi 50 m tall in a
  ring around the Cantor's Seat, visible from the Mere as a crown of stumps.
* **Unique things**: the Hushline; the sustained note; the only place enemies
  do not respawn after a boss; the one red poppy.

### 6.7 The map

Wickmere's geography is drawn by hand, not seeded. The drawing is `tools/world/atlas/atlas.json`,
and **`docs/ATLAS.md`** explains it: what each of the twenty-two provinces is, where the ranges,
rivers, lakes, woods and roads run and why, how the start is framed, how big the world is and how
full, and the story each of the map's locations could anchor. The paper map is
`docs/atlas/wickmere_atlas.png`. In one line: the Mere is at the centre, Skerrow's wall of
summits and its dales are north, the Briarwold rises east to the Thornmarch, Sedgemire's delta
drains west to the Grey Sea, Hearthvale's downs lie south of the Mere, and Cinderlea's ash is in
the south-west over the Builders' city, ending at the Hushline cliffs where the game begins.

The quests follow the map (`docs/ATLAS.md` §15 and §16). Every settlement has work given by
somebody who lives there, every place that is not a settlement is somewhere a quest sends you, and
every point of interest pays off in something the game puts there: a quest stage, a note or book
lying where its hook says, an encounter, or a Hearthstone.

### 7.1 The quiet villages (on the Roll, not on the map)

Four names the Wardens still read out, for places that can no longer be found. They are
the spine of the Wardens' line and of several books; a player can stand where the Roll
says they were and find nothing but ground.

| Name | Meaning | What the Roll says |
|---|---|---|
| **Mullbourne** | *mull* (soft soil) + *-bourne* | A mill that never worked, on a stream that moved. Went quiet first; the Roll's oldest struck entry. |
| **Harewell** | *hare* + *-well* | A hare was cut into the well lip. Nobody now living has seen the well. |
| **Larkstead** | *lark* + *-stead* | Known for its singing. The Roll notes, in a different hand, "they stopped." |
| **Gosford** | *gos* (goose) + *-ford* | Went quiet during the eleven days the Toll hummed in Y.T. 981 — the only quiet village with a date, which is why the Toll humming again in 1043 frightens the Wardens more than it frightens anyone else. |

### 7.2 The Toll's inscription

Around the crown of the Cracked Toll, in Oroth, where the bronze is thickest:

> **VAEL-OROTH · ANTHE SUL · HESK-MORN THAEL · ONDRAEL ISSE · CANTOR VAELETH · ORR**

Read word by word: *the kept note · the choir's light · the stone door of silence · the
deep water · the holder holds · bone*. The Sayers read the first four as a dedication and
the last two as a signature. The Tolling Order reads the last two as a **status report** —
present tense, still true — which is why they keep vigil and the Sayers do not. Both Briar
pamphlets quote it, and neither quotes the last word.

## 10. The main thread (seven quests)

1. **The Naming** — tutorial. You wake at the Hushline's edge with a Warden
   named Wren Tallow. Choose your name and Calling. Fight ash-wights. Reach a
   Hearthstone. Ride a cart to Merrowby.
2. **The Toll Hums** — Merrowby. The bell hums; villagers argue what it means.
   Go into the Toll's lip, find an Oroth mechanism, and the first of four
   accounts. Meet the Wardens. Learn that a hamlet went quiet.
3. **Louder Than Books** — Tollmere. The Sayers give three contradicting
   answers. Their price: the bell from the Drowned Nave.
4. **What the Water Kept** — Sedgemire. The Nave; She Who Waits; the bell. The
   reedfolk's account. Learn the Cantor's Seat is *holding* something.
5. **A Hand on the Rope** — Skerrow. The clans remember the Choir's fall; the
   Stone-Thrall King guards a name. The breath account.
6. **The Briar's Purpose** — Briarwold. The Hart of Thorns and the Standing
   Moot; the Briar was grown by the Oroth to keep the Hush *in the south*. It
   is failing. The Woodfolk's custom.
7. **The Held Note** — Cinderlea. The Tolling Order opens the Seat. The Last
   Cantor. Ending choice: **Let the note end** (the deep places quiet and empty;
   the dead rest; the Dwindling speeds, but honestly), **Hold the note** (the
   player takes the Cantor's place: the world keeps, the player's name is
   unsaid), or **Sing a new note** (requires renown ≥ 600 and a faction rank:
   the player is remembered *enough* to re-strike the bell; costs the Toll,
   which falls for good). Pass one ships all three as data with distinct epilogue
   slides and world-state flags (post-game world changes are pass two).

## 12. Themes and tone

* **Tending vs. holding.** Warmth is work done daily and in company. Grandeur
  that tries to last forever goes wrong. The villagers are right in a way the
  Builders were not; the Builders were brave in a way the villagers are not.
* **Wit with weight.** Vale dialogue is quick, gossipy and specific ("Your
  boots are muddy and the Wardens' boots are muddy; I'm just saying"). Deep
  places are silent; their lore is in objects. Cinderlea's people are kind and
  doomed and do not make a fuss about it.
* **No villains among the living.** Every faction is right about something.
  The Cantor is the antagonist and the most sympathetic figure in the game.
* **Consequences are seen, not read.** A murdered miller means a stopped mill,
  bread prices up, a black ribbon on a door, and a child who will not look at
  you.
