# WICKMERE — Game Bible (pass one)

> *"Everything that is spoken of, stays. Everything else goes quiet."* — Vale saying

Wickmere is an original open-world action RPG. It descends from three lineages
(the warmth and consequence of Fable, the freedom and systems of The Elder Scrolls,
the weight and melancholy of Dark Souls) and is built as **one game** by giving all
three a single cause: the world is slowly *dwindling*, and only what is known,
named, tended and spoken of stays warm.

This document is the design bible. `WORLD_BIBLE.md` holds the fiction (cosmology,
history, cultures, factions, bestiary, languages). `ARCHITECTURE.md` holds the code.
`DECISIONS.md` holds the reasoning behind choices. `PROGRESS.md` holds state.

---

## 1. Pillars

1. **One world, one cause.** Cozy villages, free wilds and dead deep places all
   follow from the Dwindling. Nothing is a bolted-on mode.
2. **Consequence you can see.** Morality, renown, crime and faction standing change
   your body, your prices, the greetings you hear, the doors that open.
3. **Weight.** Combat is stamina-driven and committed. Death costs something you
   can go back and reclaim.
4. **Go anywhere, improve by doing.** No invisible walls inside the bounds; skills
   grow by use; every direction from the start has something in it.
5. **Nothing reads as copy-pasted.** Every region passes the "where am I?" test in
   seconds; every named place has one thing that exists nowhere else.
6. **Data first.** Items, enemies, NPCs, quests, dialogue, factions, loot, spells,
   regions, schedules, recipes and books are data in a content pack. Code is
   systems; content is packs.

---

## 2. The one-game frame

The world is a bell that was struck once and is still ringing. The ringing fades.
Where it is loud there is warmth, colour and life; where it has faded things go
still and grey and *unsaid*. The people of Wickmere discovered long ago that
**voices renew the ringing**: names, songs, gossip, being remembered. So:

| Space | Lineage | Why it is like that |
|---|---|---|
| **Settled hearts** (villages, the lake city) | Fable | People *tend* the ringing: hearths, festivals, gossip, reputations. Warm, witty, reactive. Your renown literally matters here. |
| **The wilds between** (moor, forest, marsh, karst) | Elder Scrolls | Thin but living. Free, systemic, schedule-driven, lawless at the fringes. Improve by doing. Books and rumours disagree. |
| **The deep and old places** (barrows, mines, the drowned nave, the Choir) | Dark Souls | Where the ringing failed first, or was *held on a wrong note*. Quiet, grand, sad. Enemies stand where they fell. Shortcuts loop. Bosses are the last holders of a note. |

Every region contains all three; the mix shifts. Hearthvale is mostly heart,
Cinderlea is mostly deep. The same enemy kit reads differently by placement.

**Shrines** are *Hearthstones*: places where names are kept. Being remembered at a
Hearthstone is why you come back from death. **Death** leaves an *Echo* of you
where you fell holding all your marks (coin); another death before you reach it
lets the Echo go quiet and the marks with it. **Renown** is not vanity: it is
anchoring. A famous person is *more real*, and the world treats them so.

The player is a **Foundling**: pulled from the southern Hush with no memory but a
capacity to be *named*. Character creation is the in-fiction naming. The main
thread asks why the Cracked Toll has begun to hum, and ends at the Cantor's Seat,
where an Old Builder has held a wrong note for a thousand years.

---

## 3. Core loop

Minute: move, look, fight with stamina, loot, read, talk.
Hour: pick a direction, find a point of interest, go into the deep place beneath
it, come out changed (loot, skill, lore, renown), return to a hearth to sell,
craft, rest, be recognised.
Session: a quest thread advances, a faction rank rises, a house is bought, a
region's identity is learned, a boss falls.
Campaign: the Dwindling is understood through conflicting accounts; the player's
choices decide what note the world holds next.

---

## 4. World structure

* **World space**: one streamed overworld, 8 km × 8 km initial bounds (64 km²,
  ~56 km² land) around a central lake, the Mere. Six regions ring the lake. Edges
  are soft and in-fiction (see §4.2). The registry allows more regions to be added
  without touching systems.
* **Terrain**: Terrain3D (GDExtension) heightfield at 2 m vertex spacing, 16
  terrain regions of 2048 m. Heights, control (texture) and colour maps are
  generated by the world builder from region rules plus authored stamps
  (rivers, roads, plateaus, POI pads).
* **Cells**: 256 m cells hold placements (props, scatter, NPC spawns, POI
  scenes). A `WorldStreamer` loads a ring of cells around the player.
* **Interiors**: separate scenes ("cells") reached through doors; each has an
  owner, a story, and a `who was here / what happened` note in its data.
* **Points of interest**: ruins, shrines, standing stones, wrecks, camps, towers,
  bridges, giant bones, strange trees, waterfalls, hidden valleys. Placed with
  sightlines: each POI names at least one other POI it should be visible from.

### 4.2 Soft edges (all justified in fiction; all hinted beyond)

* North: **Windgate Pass**, closed by snow and a clan toll-house. Beyond: the
  Skerrow high country and the Ice Reach.
* East: **the Thornmarch**, the living Briar wall, grown on purpose. Beyond: the
  Hinterwood and the Oroth road.
* West: the marsh becomes open tide-flats and the **Grey Sea**. Beyond: the
  Salt Isles, whose traders sometimes reach Isseva.
* South: **the Hushline**, mist where colour and sound drain; walking in dims the
  screen and turns you around with a whisper. Beyond: what the Builders left.
* Mountains and cliffs bound the corners with real geometry, not walls.

## 5. Systems

Every system below is data-driven, save-serialisable and unit-testable. Formulas
are normative for pass one and live in `game/systems/*`.

### 5.1 Character creation (the Naming)
* Name (free text), body preset + height/bulk sliders, skin tone, head preset,
  hair style/colour, eye colour, voice set, **Calling** (background):
  Hearthkeeper, Wayfarer, Reedborn, Cragborn, Ashwalker, Lantern-Clerk.
* A Calling sets starting skill bonuses (+10 to three skills), one signature item,
  and small starting faction reputations.
* Appearance is a data record (`CharacterAppearance`) applied to a body built by
  the character forge on our own humanoid rig, so morality visuals can modify it later.

### 5.1a The opening (built — see PROGRESS "The opening")

What plays between "Be named" and the first step, on **New Game only**: never on Continue
or Load. The model is the first minutes of Fable, Oblivion and Dark Souls 2 — a voice, the
land, the stakes, then you — and it is about ninety seconds long.

* **Who speaks.** The Warden who has just asked your name (`core:npc/wren_tallow`), in
  subtitles, because pass one has no voice acting (§12). The label says *The Warden*: she
  has not told you her name yet. The Naming was her asking at the top step ("Say it clearly.
  I'll say it back and then it's done" — her `the_naming_q` line), so the opening is her
  saying it back, and then telling you, as she walks, what you have come up into.
* **Why you are where you wake.** WORLD_BIBLE §1.5: the Hushline is where the Dwindling has
  finished, and the player came out of it, which should be impossible. You came up the
  Hushline Stair (`core:poi/hushline_stair`), where Wren has stood on and off for nineteen
  years watching grey people walk *down* (her `the_hush` line). That is the whole of what she
  knows, and she says so. You stand at its head, in her camp on the rim (**The start**, below).
* **What you are shown**, each shot a slow eased move over a real place in the streamed
  world, with the time and weather that place looks like itself in:
  1. *Black.* One bell. "{name}." — "There. Said out loud, and heard. That is how it holds."
  2. *The Mere at first light*, up the Long Stride to Tollmere. The title card —
     **WICKMERE**, the bell mark, "Everything that is spoken of, stays." — inks in and out.
     "Nobody in Wickmere agrees what the world is."
  3. *Tollmere and the Sayers' Spire.* "The Sayers say it is a bell, struck once and still
     ringing. They charge by the hour to explain it."
  4. *The Drowned Nave in mist.* "The marsh says it is a tide going out."
  5. *The Fallen Hand in cold light.* "The clans say something vast is breathing in."
  6. *Merrowby under the Cracked Toll, gold morning.* "In the Vale we say it is a candle,
     and a candle wants tending."
  7. *The downs and Wardens' Rest.* "Everybody agrees what it is doing. A place nobody
     speaks of goes quiet, and then it goes. We keep their names on the Roll."
  8. *The Toll, close.* "This spring the Toll began to hum. The last time it hummed, Gosford
     went quiet in eleven days." (WORLD_BIBLE §7.1; Harewell is left for *The Toll Hums*.)
  9. *The Sunken Choir in ashfall.* "South of everything is Cinderlea, where it has gone
     furthest. South of that is the Hush, where it has finished."
  10. *The Stair*, from high over the Hush looking north at the cliff: "I have stood at the
      top of that stair nineteen years and watched grey people walk down it. Nobody has
      ever walked up it." The camera comes up over the rim into the Wardens' camp and settles
      behind your shoulder, facing north: "Then, this morning, you did." — "Keep up. I don't
      look back."
* **The hand-over.** The last shot ends exactly on the gameplay camera's pose, the letterbox
  draws back, the HUD inks in and control returns. *The Naming* (`core:quest/the_naming`)
  starts at that moment, not under the pictures, and the Warden speaks first, in her own
  greeting for the moment ("There you are. Eyes working? Good. Don't look behind you yet. Come
  to the fire."), as a line with her name on it. The first objective, *Speak to the Warden at
  her fire*, is written under the compass, and its smudge sits on the strip.
* **The start.** `core:poi/stair_head`, the Wardens' camp at the top of the Stair, on the rim
  of the Cinderlea cliff 190 m north of the Stair itself, which is a pad in the Hush at the
  cliff's foot. The POI's position is where the Foundling stands, and the camp is laid out ahead
  of it towards the Choir. The Warden stands 6 m in front at her fire, turned to you. Around
  her: two tents, a cart with its load, two grey-green Warden colours with a bell each either
  side of the way out, a signpost, lamps. Behind you: the two Oroth piers at the head of the
  stair and her Hearthstone. The stair goes from them straight down the 77 m cliff to the Hush
  and on into the water, where the mist takes it. From over the Hush it is one line climbing
  the cliff to the camp: what you came up, and what the opening's last shot flies. The first
  view is the camp, the heath beyond it, and the Choir's twelve headless colossi on the
  skyline. From the camp,
  waystones (a lamp on every third) walk 426 m north to the Choir over ground a person can
  walk, and keep more than fifty metres from anything the built world stands on the heath:
  about a minute and a half on foot, past the Cantor's Seat. The world builder places the
  heath's enemies afresh on every build, so a test holds the waystones against the spawns of
  the build on disk and names the leg to move. The Naming runs: `wake` (speak to
  the Warden, who gives you the road) → `the_choir` (walk the waystones to the Sunken Choir) →
  `ash_wights` → `hearthstone` (Pilgrim's Ash) → `the_cart`. Standing at the start completes
  nothing. The Warden is kept at her fire by her npc def's `holds`, from the moment a new game
  is named until you reach the Choir, and then she goes back to her own days.
* **The rules it keeps.** Every camera is placed relative to a place and to the ground under
  it, never at a stored height, and a test samples every path against the built terrain and
  its scatter. No shot looks south from the Hushline: the world ends 200 m past the Stair.
  The streamer follows the camera and loads the next shot while this one plays; if the
  cells are not there in time the picture holds on black with the loading caption and the
  music waits. Any key or button shows *hold to skip*; skipping arrives at exactly the state
  playing to the end does — position, streaming, time, weather, buses, nothing paused. A
  setting stops it playing on later new games, and the pause menu can replay it, after
  which everything is put back as it was.
* **Where it lives.** The shots are data, `core:cinematic/opening`
  (`content/packs/core/cinematics/opening.json`), checked by `CinematicDef` like any other
  content. `CinematicPlayer` (`systems/cinematic`) plays them in the running world, and one
  call in `GameServices.begin_new_game()` starts it. The music is `core:music/opening`,
  which `tools/audio/compose.py` composes from the same shot list so the cuts land on it.
  *Play the opening on a new game* (Settings, gameplay) turns it off, and the pause menu's
  *How it began* plays it again.
* **What it cannot fix, and the design assumes.** The Hushline Stair's own POI still stands
  where the built world put its pad: an 8 m mound in the Hush's water 100 m out from the foot
  of the cliff, with its old dressing and four ash-wights on it that nobody can reach. The stair
  that matters is the one built from the camp. The Stair Head has no pad of its own until the
  next world build (its def is dressed on the ground as it stands until then). There are no
  animals in the camp, because nothing in the asset library is one.

### 5.2 Movement & cameras
* `CharacterBody3D` with three gaits: **walk 1.8 m/s** (walk key held, or a light stick),
  **jog 5.0 m/s** (the default), **sprint 7.8 m/s** (held; stamina 8/s, §5.3; run to empty it
  stops and does not start again until a quarter of the pool is back). Sneak 1.5 m/s; locked on
  or blocking 2.6 m/s (×0.6 while blocking). Rest to a jog in 0.31 s (16 m/s², then 7 m/s² on to
  a sprint); a jog stops in 0.25 s over 0.6 m (20 m/s²), a sprint in 0.48 s over 2.1 m
  (12 m/s² down to a jog). Sprint dodge-roll.
* The legs keep pace with the ground. Each gait has its own clip made at its speed (Walk,
  Run for the jog, Sprint, Sneak_Walk). All of them share one stride phase and are played at
  ground speed over stride (0.5–1.6 times as made), so a planted foot stays planted through
  every blend between them. Upright gaits ride the hips a few centimetres below standing; only
  the sneak crouches.
* Movement is relative to the view: W is where the camera looks, on the ground; S away, A and D
  to its sides. The body turns to where it is going at a limited rate (900°/s standing, 720 at a
  walk, 540 at a jog, 300 at a sprint, easing into the last few degrees) and gives up speed for a
  large turn, so a reversal plants and turns instead of moonwalking. Locked on, blocking or in
  first person it faces the target or the view and strafes. The camera turns with the mouse and
  never with the body; the compass reads the camera.
* First-person (arms visible) and third-person (orbit, shoulder offset, lock-on
  framing). `V` / right-stick click toggles. Third person sits 3.6 m behind a pivot 1.55 m up
  and 0.4 m over the shoulder, and follows with a gentle lag (about 0.36 m at a jog). A sprint
  draws it back 0.5 m and widens the view 7°, eased in and out. Collision pulls the camera in at
  once and lets it back out over about 0.35 s. Physics is interpolated, so bodies moved at 60 Hz
  are smooth at any refresh rate; the camera, the compass and anything moved per frame read the
  interpolated body.
* Jump is modest (1.1 m); climbing is via placed ladders and mantle on low ledges.

### 5.3 Combat
* **Stamina** (`100 + 8·Endurance`) drains per action: light attack 18, heavy 32,
  dodge 22, block-hit = damage·0.6·(1−stability), sprint 8/s. Regen 30/s after 0.8 s.
* **Attacks** are committed: input buffered 0.25 s, cancel only into dodge after the
  active frames. Chains of up to 3 lights; heavies charge.
* **Hitboxes** are weapon-defined swing volumes (the weapon's reach long, shin to crown
  high) active on animation frames (`hit_start`, `hit_end` per attack, from the attack's
  clip scaled by the weapon's speed; see DECISIONS 2026-09-22).
* **Lock-on**: targets in a 30 m cone, cycle with stick flick / mouse wheel;
  camera frames both; hard-lock movement strafes.
* **Dodge**: 0.6 s roll, i-frames 0.08–0.38 s. Heavy load lengthens it and cuts
  i-frames.
* **Block**: shield/weapon `stability` (0..1) scales damage and stamina cost.
* **Parry**: block pressed within 0.18 s before a hit with a parry-capable item
  → attacker enters `riposte_open` for 2 s; riposte deals 3× damage.
* **Poise**: each entity has `poise_max`; attacks deal `poise_damage`; poise regens
  4/s after 1.5 s. At 0 → stagger (`Hit_A`), poise resets. Heavy weapons deal
  more; hyper-armour frames on heavies ignore poise damage below a threshold.
* **Damage** = `weapon_base · skill_mult(1 + skill/200) · attack_mult · charge`
  − `armour_flat` then `· (1 − resist_type)`, minimum 1. Crit (backstab, riposte,
  sneak attack) multiplies before armour.
* **Ranged**: bows have draw time; arrows are items with physics arc; aiming pulls
  camera in. Crossbows: no draw, slow reload.
* **Magic** ("Saying"): mana = `60 + 6·Will`. Five schools: Kindling (fire, light),
  Hush (frost, silence, stealth), Binding (wards, shields, snares), Mending
  (heal, cleanse), Calling (summon, animate). Spells are data with cast type
  (projectile, self, aura, target, summon), cost, cast time, school, effects.
* **Status effects**: burning, chilled (slow), webbed (slow, from the Briarwold
  weavers), silenced (no casting), bleeding, poisoned, quieted (renown drain in
  deep places), stagger, knockdown.

### 5.4 Enemies & AI
* Archetypes (data): `brute`, `skirmisher`, `pack`, `charger`, `ambusher`,
  `caster`, `sentinel`, `swarm`, `elite`, `boss`. Each has perception
  (sight cone, hearing radius), behaviour parameters (engage range, circle
  tendency, retreat threshold, patience), attack set with telegraph times.
* Perception feeds stealth (§5.11). States: idle/schedule → suspicious → search →
  combat → return. Enemies in deep places **do not chase past their threshold** and
  reset when the player rests at a Hearthstone.
* Placement is authored per POI as encounter data: positions, facing, alert
  groups, ambush triggers, patrol paths. The rule: every encounter is a small
  puzzle (a caster behind a brute, an ambusher above a chokepoint).
* Bosses: phased data (`phases[]` with hp thresholds, added attacks, arena
  changes), a fog gate, a name card, a bespoke arena scene, a unique drop.

### 5.5 Hearthstones, death & Echoes
* Resting at a Hearthstone: full restore, respawn point set, refills flask charges,
  resets non-boss enemies in deep places, advances time optionally.
* On death: an **Echo** node is placed at the death position with all marks;
  saved in the save file. Player respawns at last Hearthstone. Touching the Echo
  restores marks. Dying again with an Echo present: the old Echo is lost.
* Echoes can be seen from a distance (a faint standing figure with a light).

### 5.6 Progression
* Skills (16): One-Handed, Two-Handed, Archery, Block, Armour, Sneak, Speech,
  Alchemy, Smithing, Enchanting, Athletics, Kindling, Hush, Binding, Mending,
  Calling. (Calling and Mending share XP with use of their spells.)
* Use grants skill XP; XP to next skill level = `40 · 1.12^level`. Skill gains add
  level progress; character level `L` requires `sum_skill_gains ≥ 10·L + 5·L²`.
* Each level: +1 attribute (Vigour → HP, Endurance → stamina/load, Will → mana)
  and one perk point. Perks are data, gated by skill thresholds.
* Attributes: Vigour, Endurance, Will, plus derived Load.

### 5.7 Inventory, equipment, loot
* Items are data: id, name, category, weight, value, stack, tags, description
  (lore-bearing, every unique item has one), model, icon, stats/effects.
* Slots: main hand, off hand, head, body, hands, feet, ring ×2, amulet, quick
  slots ×4. Load affects dodge and stamina regen.
* Loot tables: weighted entries with conditions (region, luck, quest state),
  nested tables, guaranteed drops. Containers have ownership.
* Equipment is visible on the body (attachment to `handslot.l/r`, `head`, `chest`).

### 5.8 Crafting, alchemy, enchanting
* **Smithing** at forges: recipes as data, consume materials, skill gates, temper
  existing gear (+10% per tier).
* **Alchemy** at alembics or in the field: each ingredient has 4 effects; the
  first is known, the rest are discovered by eating or combining. Two ingredients
  sharing an effect make a potion; magnitude scales with skill.
* **Enchanting** at Name-tables: disenchant to learn; enchant using **Ember Motes**
  (captured from slain foes with a Kindling spell or found) as charge. Charge
  depletes; recharge with motes.

### 5.9 Dialogue
* Node graph per NPC/topic: `lines` with `speaker`, `text`, `conditions`,
  `effects`, `choices`, `next`. Conditions read quests, factions, morality,
  renown, skills, items, time, personality traits. Effects set flags, give items,
  move quests, change reputation.
* Greeting lines are chosen from a personality × standing matrix: villagers react
  to renown tier, Hearth/Hollow, faction rank, crime, and to what you're wearing.
* **Gestures** (bow, wave, rude, dance, laugh, flex) are inputs that raise or
  lower disposition based on personality; NPCs answer with gestures and lines.

### 5.10 Quests, factions, reputation
* Quest = stages with objectives (talk, reach, kill, collect, deliver, escort,
  choice), on-enter/on-complete effects, journal text, markers (optional, and
  the map never spoon-feeds: markers are approximate "regions of interest").
* Layers: **main thread** (7 quests), **faction lines** (Wardens of the Hearth,
  Sayers' Circle, the Quiet Hands, the Tolling Order: 3–4 quests each), **side
  quests** (10+), **radiant**: job boards generate bounties/hunts/deliveries from
  templates with region-appropriate targets and rewards.
* Factions: rank thresholds, reputation −100..100, joinable flag, rivals
  (joining one lowers another), law authority per region.

### 5.11 Morality & renown
* **Hearth ↔ Hollow** axis −100..100 from deeds (help/harm, steal/give, murder,
  mercy). **Renown** 0..1000 from quests, kills of named things, bosses, deeds
  witnessed (spread through gossip: witnessed deeds propagate to the settlement's
  rumour pool over hours).
* Visible on the body: Hearth tiers add warm skin, golden eye glint, a faint halo
  in darkness, fireflies at rest; Hollow tiers add pallor, dark veins, red eyes,
  small horns, crows. Renown tiers add a title in greetings, crowds gathering,
  bards' verses, discounts or fear.
* World reactions: greeting pools, prices ±25%, guard tolerance, children follow
  or flee, dogs growl, doors barred in Hollow villages, shrines glow brighter.

### 5.12 NPC schedules & personalities
* Each NPC: home cell, workplace, a weekly timetable of `(day filter, hour,
  location, activity)`; activities: sleep, work, eat, idle, pray, socialise,
  patrol. Off-screen NPCs are simulated abstractly (position by schedule).
* Personality traits (brave/timid, greedy/generous, pious/cynical, gossip/quiet,
  proud/humble) select lines, gesture responses, crime reactions, and prices.

### 5.13 Crime, bounty, stealth
* Ownership on items, containers, doors, beds, animals. Taking owned items,
  trespassing, assault, murder, pickpocketing → crime events with severity.
* Witnesses: NPCs with line of sight and awareness report; gossip spreads bounty
  to the region's **law faction** (differs per region; Briarwold has none,
  Skerrow uses blood-price). Guards confront: pay, jail (time passes; skill
  practice lost), resist. Bounty decays slowly in lawless regions.
* Stealth: visibility from light level at the player, movement noise, crouch,
  armour weight; detection meter per observer; sneak attacks ×3 (dagger ×6);
  pickpocket chance from Sneak vs target awareness; lockpicking minigame-lite
  (timed sweet spot) on doors/chests.

### 5.14 Economy, shops, property, jobs
* Merchants: stock lists with restock hours, gold pool, buy categories.
  Price = `base · region_mod · supply_mod · disposition_mod · (1.3 − Speech/300)`.
* Property: houses with deed prices in each settlement; owning gives a key,
  storage, a bed, a Hearthstone-lite, and rent income if let. Furnishings bought.
* Jobs: radiant job boards; simple timed jobs at stations (chop, smith, brew)
  paying marks and skill XP.

### 5.15 Time, weather, atmosphere
* 1 game day = 48 real minutes (configurable). Sun/moon, colour ramps per hour.
* Weather per region: weighted states (clear, overcast, rain, fog, storm, snow,
  ashfall) with transitions; affects light, fog density, wetness, NPC schedules
  (rain sends villagers indoors), stealth (rain masks noise).
* Region post-processing: fog colour, tint, saturation, vignette; music and
  ambience layers crossfade on region change and by time/danger.

### 5.16 Map, compass, HUD, menus
* Compass shows cardinal points, discovered locations only, quest *areas* not
  pins. Map is a painted, partially revealed chart: you fill it by looking from
  high places (surveying at vistas) and by buying charts.
* HUD: health/stamina/mana bars, quick slots, lock-on reticle, subtle prompts.
  Hides when idle. Everything themed (see §7).
* Menus: inventory (paper-doll), journal (quests, rumours, bestiary, books read),
  map, skills/perks, settings (video, audio, controls with rebinding, gameplay),
  save/load slots.

### 5.17 Books
* Readable in-world books (data): title, author, body (markdown-lite), optional
  skill teaching, optional quest hook. Accounts conflict on purpose.

## 7. Art direction — "Storybook Painted": self-made, mid-poly, strong light

Every asset in Wickmere is made by this project's own tools (Blender scripted
headlessly, Python image synthesis, Godot shaders). No third-party model or
texture packs are used. The look is **not low-poly**: it is a painted storybook
realism, closer to an illustrated fairy-tale than to either photoreal or
flat-shaded minimalism.

### 7.0 The style contract (binding for every asset)

1. **Geometry**: smooth-shaded, mid-poly with modelled bevels and real
   silhouettes (a barrel has staves and hoops, a roof has individual tile rows,
   a tree has a Sapling-grown trunk and branches). Typical prop 500–6 000
   triangles, hero pieces up to 40 000, characters 8–15 000, with generated LODs.
2. **Surfaces**: hand-painted-style PBR textures baked by our own procedural
   material library (Blender node materials baked with Cycles: albedo, normal,
   roughness, optional height). Painterly means: soft colour variation, visible
   large-scale strokes and edge wear, restrained micro-detail, no photographic
   grain. One material library (`tools/forge/materials.py`) is shared by every
   generator so wood, stone, plaster, iron, cloth, bark and leaf read the same in
   every region; regions change the palette, not the language.
3. **Colour is the region's voice.** Each region has a 6-colour palette and a
   light recipe (sun colour/angle, fog colour/density, ambient tint,
   saturation). Generators take the region palette as input, so the same
   fence generator yields Hearthvale oak or Briarwold black-ash.
4. **Light does the mood.** Sun, sky, height fog, baked vertex AO, subtle SSAO
   where available, bloom on emissives, ACES/filmic tonemap; god-rays only in
   the Briarwold and the Drowned Nave.
5. **Characters**: original bodies built by our character forge (procedural
   base mesh, proportion sliders, heads, hair, clothing shells) on our own rig;
   animations authored by code from pose libraries and gait generators, then
   refined by review renders. Creatures use their own generated rigs and gaits.
6. **Variation by construction**: every generator is seeded and takes
   parameters (age, wear, wealth, region); instances also get per-instance
   colour/scale/rotation jitter. No two placed trees, rocks or houses are the
   same mesh unless they are deliberately a matched set.
7. **UI and icons**: our own drawn parchment/brass theme (Python-generated
   textures) and item icons rendered in-engine from the meshes with one light
   rig. Fonts are the only third-party files (SIL Open Font License), listed in
   `LICENSES.md`.

A `tools/style_report.py` pass lists every material and texture in the project
that is not produced by the forge; the build fails the style gate if any remain
unlisted in `STYLE_EXCEPTIONS.md`.

## 10. Uniqueness tests (enforced by review + automated checks)

1. **Region drop test**: screenshots from random points per region, HUD off,
   must be sortable by region by a reviewer. Automated proxy in
   `tools/uniqueness_check.py`, which scores two axes separately: **colour**
   (palette histograms) and **landform** (skyline profile, ruggedness, where
   the detail sits down the frame -- computed on per-image-normalised luminance,
   so it is blind to tint, and deliberately blind to the absolute horizon height
   and the sky fraction, which are the photographer's choices and not the
   place's). Leave-one-out nearest-centroid accuracy on the two together must
   reach 0.80; landform alone must reach 0.55. The second bar exists because a
   region that separates on colour alone is a region that would vanish if you
   desaturated it, and a filter is not a place.
   **The landform bar only binds at six or more images per region.** Below that,
   leave-one-out over six regions turns on one or two frames and the figure lands
   anywhere, including below the chance line; it is printed and explained and is
   not allowed to fail the run, and the confusion pairs are the part worth
   reading. Never steer the world by a number the sample cannot support.
2. **Named-place test**: each named location's data declares its `unique_feature`;
   the check fails if any two locations share one.
3. **Interior test**: every interior declares `resident`, `trade`, `wealth`,
   `story` and `unique_object`; no two interiors may share `unique_object`.
4. **Dungeon test**: every deep place declares `formed_by` and at least one
   shortcut that loops back.

## 11. Technical budgets (measured by `tools/perf_probe`)

* Streaming ring: 3×3 cells full detail, 5×5 reduced, terrain by Terrain3D LOD.
* ≤ 2 000 draw calls, ≤ 1.5 M triangles in view at 1080p on the reference
  (integrated GPU class). Cell load ≤ 8 ms per frame budget via threaded
  instancing.
* Save ≤ 1 MB typical, load ≤ 2 s.
